# 41 | Data protection: what the system does against the obligations of Middle East personal-data laws

The mentor's requirement (2026-09-27): the application must respect Middle East data-protection laws such as the Saudi PDPL. The Gulf states each have their own law (Saudi Arabia PDPL, UAE Federal Decree-Law 45 of 2021, Qatar Law 13 of 2016, and others), but they share the same families of obligation. This page maps each family to **what the Rust build does today, where the evidence is, and what is not built**. It is an engineering mapping, not legal advice; a lawyer for the target country decides the legal basis and the exact wording of notices.

Data in scope: health-insurance claims. They name a patient, a member, a provider, diagnoses and services: personal and health data, which these laws treat as sensitive.

| Obligation family | What the system does | Evidence | Not built / honest limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Data stays in the country / limits on cross-border transfer** | Everything runs on the hospital's own servers: API, worker, database, cache, and the AI model (vLLM, local). Only the proxy publishes ports; the database and cache sit on a network with no route out. The rule engine and the AI step make no outside calls. The only optional outside call is an RFC 3161 time-stamp request, which sends a hash of the log head, never data. | `deploy/rust/compose.yml`, `deploy_attack.py` (50 checks: isolation, ports, egress), `docs/35`, `docs/40` | Cloud model providers exist in the Python build only; the Rust build refuses to use them. Running it on the real machine is not done yet. |
| **Data minimisation** | Identifiers are masked by default: a reviewer sees a stable pseudonym instead of the patient and member id. Unmasking needs a separate permission, a written reason, is logged, and is limited to 60 claims per person per hour; the reason may not itself contain the identifier. The AI never sees the claim's values (it is given placeholders; the values are filled in afterwards and checked). | `docs/39` 11.2, `pentest.py` privacy checks, `cg-access/src/masking.rs` | Identifiers are still stored in the claim document (encrypted at rest); the keyed `patient_key` index exists so a patient's earlier claims can be found. |
| **Purpose limitation and access control** | Four levels (viewer, reviewer, senior reviewer, administrator) with per-permission flags, enforced on the server, never only by hiding a button. An administrator cannot read or decide claims; a reviewer cannot decide a high-severity finding; the person who signs cannot countersign their own decision. | `docs/39` (authorization matrix in the pen test), mutation tests | The legal purpose and basis are decided by the insurer or hospital, outside the software. |
| **Security safeguards** | Encrypted in transit (TLS, HSTS). Encrypted at rest: the claim data, the people's names and the audit logs, with keys that can be rotated. Two-factor sign-in (password and authenticator), session tokens that can be revoked, throttling and lock-out that cannot be used to lock the owner out, cross-site request refusal. Tamper-evident audit: hash chain, signed anchor, a second append-only copy, an optional outside time stamp. Two-person approval of the most dangerous administrator actions. Encrypted backups. | `docs/39` sections 1 to 12 (116-check pen test, 75 mutants, fuzzing, real-model red team, browser attack test), `docs/40` | Not penetration-tested by an outside party. Not run on the target machine. Key custody (who holds `.env`) is an organisational control. |
| **Accuracy and correction** | A claim is never edited: a correction is a new version, checked again from scratch. Every reviewer decision is an entry in a signed, chained log, so a wrong decision is answered by a new one, with the old one still visible. | `docs/31`, `docs/38` | There is no screen for a patient or member to ask for a correction; that goes through the insurer's process. |
| **Rights of the person: access, correction, deletion, objection** | The pseudonym and the keyed `patient_key` let all claims of one person be found without the identifier being readable. The audit logs hold badge numbers and claim ids, never the patient's name or identifier (reasons that contain the identifier are refused). | `cg-mongo/src/crypt.rs`, `masking.rs`, `cg-queue/src/privacy.rs` | **Built as operator commands** (`cg queue privacy-export`, `privacy-erase --confirm N`; docs/58): export of everything held about one person, and erasure of every version of their claims (refused while someone is working on a claim; logged). The audit log keeps claim ids only, so it needs no rewriting, which matters because the log is deliberately not editable. **Not built:** a web route, a second administrator's approval, erasure from backups. |
| **Retention (keep only as long as needed)** | Short-lived data expires on its own (sign-in counters, used codes, revoked tokens, approval requests). | store indexes, `privacy.rs` | **Built, off by default:** `RETENTION_DAYS` and an hourly purge job for decided claims (docs/58). **Not decided:** how long; that is the insurer's call. Logs hold no patient data and are kept as long as the auditor requires. |
| **Breach detection and notification** | Detection: the audit chain, its mirror and the time stamp raise an alarm on any rewrite; the health panel shows it; failed sign-ins, forbidden requests, unmask counts and approval events are all logged and countable. | `docs/39`, `docs/40` section 8 (incident playbooks), `audit_guard_demo.py` | Notification of the authority and the people affected is a process, not software. The runbook says what to collect; it does not send anything. |
| **Records of processing and impact assessment** | This page, `docs/32` (privacy and security note), `docs/39`, `docs/40`, and the specification list what is processed, why, by whom, and how it is protected. | the documents | A formal data-protection impact assessment and a named data-protection officer belong to the organisation that runs it. |
| **Processors and sub-processors** | There are none: no third-party service receives data in the deployed system (the model, the database and the cache are all local). | `compose.yml` | The optional time-stamp authority receives a hash only. |

## What to say if asked "are you compliant?"
No software is "compliant" by itself: the law binds the organisation that runs it. What this system gives that organisation is: data that never leaves its servers, minimal exposure of identifiers to staff, strong authentication, encryption at rest and in transit, a tamper-evident record of who did what, and a short list of obligations it still has to meet around the software. The two gaps in that list that need code are **subject access and erasure** and **retention**; both are designed here and unbuilt.
